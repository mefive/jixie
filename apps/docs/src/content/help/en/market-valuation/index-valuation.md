# View index valuation

The Valuation page shows trailing price-to-earnings, price-to-book, static price-to-earnings, and turnover for an index, then compares each current value with that index's own history.

## Open the Valuation page

1. Select **Valuation** in the top navigation.
2. Confirm the index name and code at the upper left.
3. Check the **Data updated through** date.
4. Wait for four metric cards and the historical chart.

The numbered areas are:

1. Index selector and data date.
2. Current values, ten-year percentiles, and all-history percentiles.
3. Metric and time-range controls.
4. Valuation metric and index close.
5. Percentile method.

![Main areas of the index Valuation page](/docs/images/help/zh/market-valuation/valuation-overview-01.png)

The valuation update date can differ slightly from the Market page. Use the date shown on the Valuation page.

## Change the index

1. Select the index field at the upper left.
2. Choose an index from the list.
3. Wait for the name, four cards, and chart to update.

The numbered areas are:

1. Current index.
2. Available index names and codes.

![Change the valuation index](/docs/images/help/zh/market-valuation/valuation-index-01.png)

Indices differ in industry, size, and earnings composition. Similar P/E values do not make two indices equivalent. Compare each one with its own history and understand its constituents.

## The four metrics

### Trailing P/E

$$
\text{Trailing P/E}
=
\frac{\text{Index market value}}{\text{Constituent earnings over the latest 12 months}}
$$

Trailing earnings update as new reports arrive. Some dates may have no valid value when aggregate earnings are negative or insufficient.

### Price-to-book

$$
\text{P/B}
=
\frac{\text{Index market value}}{\text{Constituent book equity}}
$$

Normal ranges can differ substantially among financial, cyclical, and asset-light industries.

### Static P/E

Static P/E uses completed annual earnings. Its difference from trailing P/E comes from a different earnings period; neither is automatically more accurate.

### Turnover

Turnover describes how actively the constituents traded that day. It is not a valuation multiple and cannot by itself indicate expensive or cheap.

## Use the historical chart

1. Select trailing P/E, P/B, static P/E, or turnover above the chart.
2. Select five years, ten years, or all history.
3. Read the dark valuation line and the light index-close line.
4. Drag the lower zoom control to inspect a shorter interval.

2. Current metrics and two percentile ranges.
3. Metric selector.
4. Time-range selector.
5. Valuation line, index-close line, and zoom control.

The two lines use different vertical axes. Do not treat an intersection as a buy or sell point, and do not read index points as valuation multiples.

## Common questions

### A metric displays a dash

No valid value may exist for that date. Check the update date and another index. Do not fill the gap with a nearby day's value.

### Ten-year and all-history percentiles differ substantially

Their comparison periods differ. Long-term changes in earnings structure and valuation regimes can create a large gap.

### Does a low P/E prove undervaluation?

No. P/E can be temporarily low when earnings are near a cyclical peak. Also consider earnings quality, industry composition, and multiple historical windows.
